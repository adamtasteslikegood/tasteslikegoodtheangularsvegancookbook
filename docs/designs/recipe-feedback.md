## Problem Statement

The site publishes AI-generated vegan recipes. Nothing on a recipe page shows that a person cooked it. That is a trust gap for readers. The SEO driver review also holds, as one of three unproven hypotheses, that Google spends little crawl on pages it expects to be low-value AI content. Phase 1 does not measure any search effect; it measures whether cooks leave entries. The 2026-10-03 review first proposed that Adam test 10 to 15 recipes himself. Adam's correction: "the site is primarily for AI generated recipes, needs a way for people to leave actual feedback on recipes they generated OR cooked - whether me or friends, family or user."

## Demand Evidence

- Feedback already exists. People who cook the recipes tell Adam directly, by text, call or in person.
- What they say is mostly "it worked and I liked it." Adam: "B is the honest answer."
- Adam rejected the assumption that AI recipes mainly draw error reports: "your bet about ai recipes is wrong."
- Not yet evidenced: a specific named person and their words; any feedback from someone who does not know Adam. Search brings 27 impressions per 28 days, so strangers are few.

## Status Quo

Feedback reaches one person, Adam, through private messages. It is not attached to the recipe, the next cook never sees it, and it depends on knowing the founder.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Target user: the friends and family who already cook these recipes and report back, then signed-in users who save a public recipe.

A narrower wedge was offered and declined: a cooked mark and optional note only, signed in, with stars and the Google rating later. Adam chose the full feature instead. If RCP-119 capacity is short, the separable parts are, in order: the JSON-LD rating, the author's-notes display, and the star average (leaving the cooked count and notes).

Wedge Adam chose: the full feature (a "cooked it" entry with a star rating and an optional "what I changed" note, with the rating shown to Google once it is real), closed to guests, behind an eligibility gate. Adam on the note: "if someone has the type of feedback where they adjusted things and had better results there needs to be a section/mechanism for that."

## Constraints

- Public `/r/<slug>` pages are server-rendered by Flask and have no signed-in UI. They should stay that way.
- No ads, no tracking, no life story. Feedback must stay short and must not add analytics.
- Guests (localStorage sessions) cannot leave feedback.
- Backend changes ship through the release train: Backend PR, pointer bump, tag. One Alembic head.
- Solo founder. Moderation must fit in a queue one person reads.

## Premises

Agreed by Adam (2026-10-04):

1. Feedback exists today and has no home. The feature moves it onto the recipe.
2. Eligibility is: signed in, account at least 24 hours old, and the recipe saved to the cook's own cookbook. Adam first proposed requiring a published recipe on the commenter's account plus a 14 to 30 day wait. The published-recipe bar was dropped because roughly two accounts would qualify and the cooks who give feedback today would be excluded. The long wait was dropped at approval; Adam: "making people wait 14 days is unrealistic and unnecessary with the other - save recipe to your account etc - moderation safeguards."
3. A rating on your own recipe does not count toward the public score. It shows as author's notes.
4. No rating is sent to Google until a recipe has at least 3 ratings from different non-author accounts.
5. The feedback section does not render a count or an empty box on a recipe with no entries.
6. Written notes from an account are held for Adam's approval until that account has one approved note. Stars and the cooked mark are stored and counted immediately.

Account age is the same for everyone: **24 hours** from account creation, for your own recipes and for other people's (Adam, at approval: "24 hours for everyone, no 14 day wait time").

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Feedback on the saved copy, summary on the public page (chosen)

The control lives on the cook's saved copy in the app. The public page shows a read-only summary and a link that explains how to add feedback.

### Approach B: Feedback written directly on the public page

Ruled out for now: it puts session state and a write surface on public pages that have neither today, and builds report, reply and moderation tooling before any entry exists.

### Approach C: A private cook log that feeds the public page

Not chosen: sharing would be opt-in, so public proof arrives slowly. Its best idea (repeat cooks as a signal) is kept as an open question.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A, with two additions from Adam.

**Phase 1 (RCP-119 candidate)**

Items marked (proposed) were this design's defaults. Adam approved the doc on 2026-10-04 and changed two of them: account age is 24 hours for everyone, and moderation adds email notifications. Required stars, first name only, and entries surviving a deleted saved copy stand as proposed.

Entry and identity

- An entry is: "I cooked this", a required 1 to 5 star rating (proposed), and an optional "what I changed" note with a length cap. One entry per account per public recipe. Because stars are required, "Cooked by N", the rating count and the JSON-LD `ratingCount` are the same number.
- The owner of an entry can edit it and can delete it. Deleting recomputes the recipe's count, average and threshold.
- Target recipe: an entry is keyed to a public recipe's id. A saved copy resolves to its nearest published ancestor through `Recipe.source_recipe_id`. A copy that has itself been published is its own target, and its owner is its author.
- Where the control lives: for a cook, on their saved copy; for an author, on their own published recipe in the app.
- If a saved copy has no published ancestor (older copies with no source, or the source was unpublished), the control does not render.
- A saved copy made as a guest must keep its link to the public source through the guest-to-login merge. That, and a data check of existing saved copies, are inputs to the eng review.
- Before submitting, the control states that the rating, the note and the cook's first name will be shown publicly on the recipe page. Name form: first name only (proposed).
- Non-goal for Phase 1: feedback on recipes that are not published. Adam's "generated OR cooked" is covered by author's notes on your own published recipe and cooked entries on other people's.

Eligibility, checked on the server at submission

- A cook (non-author) must be signed in, have an account at least 24 hours old, and have a saved copy of the recipe.
- An author needs only to be signed in with an account at least 24 hours old. No saved copy applies.
- A signed-in cook who has saved the recipe but is not yet eligible sees the control disabled, with the date it opens.

Entry lifecycle

- An entry survives deletion of the saved copy (proposed): it records that the cook made the dish. When a cook deletes a saved copy that has an entry, they are asked whether to keep or delete the entry. Saving the same recipe again shows the existing entry and its note status, not an empty control.
- Deleting the account removes that account's entries and the numbers are recomputed.
- Entries are kept when a recipe is unpublished and return if it is published again. Deleting the recipe deletes its entries. Editing a public recipe leaves existing ratings in place; that is an accepted limit for Phase 1.

Moderation

- The rule is per account, not per entry: until an account has one approved note, every new or edited note from it is held. The stars and cooked mark on that entry count meanwhile. Author's notes follow the same rule.
- A rejected note is not shown; the entry's stars stay; the cook sees the note's status on their copy; the account stays unapproved, so its next note is held too.
- Once an account has an approved note, its later notes and edits publish without review. Adam can list every note and edit that published without review, in the same place as the approval queue.
- Adam can hide a note or a whole entry at any time. Hiding a note leaves the entry's stars counting. Hiding an entry removes it from the count and the average. The cook sees the hidden status. Editing a hidden note or entry does not put it back; only Adam can.
- Hiding a note returns its account to the held state, so a repeat offender is reviewed again.
- Queue, list and hide action live in an Adam-only view in the app. Email is notification only (Adam, at approval): one email when a new note joins the queue, and a reminder of pending notes on a daily or every-other-day schedule. Emails link into the app view; they carry no approve or reject links. The server refuses approve, reject and hide for every other account; how Adam is identified on the server is an input to the eng review.

Public recipe page

- Requirement: the feedback section and the JSON-LD reflect a new entry, a hide, a rejection or a threshold change on the next request. These pages returned `Cache-Control: private` when probed on 2026-10-03; the eng review confirms nothing else caches them.
- No entries of any kind: one link line only, "Add feedback". No count, no empty box.
- Author's entry only: author's notes, with the author's stars shown inside that block, and the link line.
- 1 or 2 non-author entries: "Cooked by N" and any approved notes with the cook's first name. No stars, no average.
- 3 or more non-author entries: "Cooked by N", the average rating, approved notes newest first with the cook's first name.
- The author's entry is always outside the count and the average.
- "Add feedback" explains the path in Adam's words: "to comment on this recipe log-in/create account and save the recipe to your cookbook, then comment there", and says that new accounts can comment after 24 hours. It leads through the existing Save to cookbook call to action.
- Recipe JSON-LD emits `aggregateRating` only at 3 or more non-author entries, computed in the same request as the visible numbers.

**Phase 2 or 3 (planned, not in the first build; Adam's request)**

- Saved copies show the feedback from their public source recipe and stay updated as new entries are published, with an option to mute it.
- Edit history, report-abuse, author replies.

## Success Criteria

Read 4 weeks after Phase 1 ships:

- At least 10 entries from at least 5 accounts that are not Adam's.
- At least 1 recipe past the 3-entry threshold, with `aggregateRating` valid in the Rich Results Test.
- At least 1 approved "what I changed" note.
- Zero spam or abusive notes published.
- No recipe page shows an empty feedback box.

Also record, for the named cooks invited through The Assignment, from existing account and saved-recipe records (no analytics are added): signed in; saved the recipe; became eligible and opened the saved copy after the control existed; left an entry. If fewer than 5 non-Adam accounts leave an entry, that funnel says where they stopped. Cooks who never signed in or saved point at friction in the gate. Cooks who were eligible, saw the control and left nothing point at supply.

Phase 2 is released when all five criteria above are met.

## Reviewer Concerns

Disposition: CONCERNS_RECORDED

Stop: MAX_ITERATIONS

### R3-1 — consistency

**Problem**

> The Assignment asks all five people to 'save one recipe they have all eaten' so that one recipe reaches three cooks, but an entry is defined as 'I cooked this' and the public page says 'Cooked by N'. Someone who ate the dish at Adam's table and then leaves an entry makes a false cooked claim, and the first aggregateRating sent to Google would rest on those entries.

**Remedy**

> Change The Assignment to a recipe that at least three of the five have each cooked themselves (or ask three of them to cook one named recipe before launch), and keep 'eaten' out of the path to an entry.

### R3-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> The diagram was not updated with the text. It says the server resolves 'saved copy -> root public recipe' while Phase 1 now commits to the nearest published ancestor, and its consent line says 'name' while Phase 1 commits to 'first name'.

**Remedy**

> Update the diagram to 'nearest published ancestor' and 'first name'.

### R3-3 — completeness

**Problem**

> The rule 'a copy that has itself been published is its own target, and its owner is its author' leaves the cook's relation to the original recipe undefined. A cook who left an entry on the original from a saved copy and then publishes that copy now has a control that targets their own recipe as author. Whether the earlier entry on the original stays, how the cook edits or deletes it, and whether that cook can still rate the original at all, are not stated.

**Remedy**

> State what happens to an existing entry on the ancestor when the copy is published (recommended: it stays and remains editable and deletable by its owner) and where the owner reaches it.

### R3-4 — completeness

**Problem**

> An entry's owner 'can edit it and can delete it', but the only surface for that is the control on the saved copy, and 'if a saved copy has no published ancestor (... or the source was unpublished), the control does not render'. Entries are kept through an unpublish and return on republish, so while the source is unpublished the cook cannot delete an entry that will go public again, and cannot see a held, rejected or hidden status.

**Remedy**

> State that a saved copy with an existing entry still shows that entry with its status and a delete action when the source is unpublished, even though no new entry can be made.

### R3-5 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The funnel says its counts come 'from existing account and saved-recipe records (no analytics are added)', but one of its steps is 'became eligible and opened the saved copy after the control existed'. Account and saved-recipe records do not record that a cook opened a saved copy. The step that separates 'did not see the control' from 'saw it and left nothing' therefore has no data source, and the 'point at supply' reading depends on it.

**Remedy**

> Either say that Adam asks the named invited cooks directly whether they opened the saved copy and saw the control, or drop that step and state that the funnel cannot separate the two cases.

### R3-6 — clarity

**Problem**

> 'Hiding a note returns its account to the held state' does not say what happens to that account's other notes that are already published, nor whether hiding a whole entry (rather than a note) has the same effect. It also does not say whether approving one later note returns the account to publishing without review.

**Remedy**

> State that already-published notes from the account stay up unless Adam hides them (or are held again), that hiding an entry has the same effect on the account as hiding a note, and that one newly approved note restores the approved state.

### R3-7 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The proposed 'Adam-only view in the app' for the queue, the list and the hide action assumes the app can tell Adam from any other signed-in user. The document does not say whether an admin role or flag exists today or has to be built, and the server must enforce it on approve, reject and hide, not only hide the view. This changes the size of Phase 1.

**Remedy**

> Add to the eng review inputs: how Adam is identified on the server (existing role, or a new one), and state that approve, reject and hide are refused for every other account.

### R3-8 — clarity

**Problem**

> The page states do not cover the author's block fully. 'Author's notes follow the same rule', so an author's first note is held, but the 'Author's entry only' state does not say what renders while that note is held or after it is rejected (stars alone, or nothing). The '1 or 2' and '3 or more' states also do not mention the author's block; only the diagram shows 'Author's notes' beside non-author entries.

**Remedy**

> State what the author's block shows when the author's note is held or rejected, and add the author's block to the two non-author page states.
