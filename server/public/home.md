# How to Use VeganGenius Chef: From Craving to Cookbook

Updated: October 12, 2026

You don’t need to know what to search for—or how to write a clever AI prompt. Tell VeganGenius Chef what sounds good, what you have, or what you want to learn to cook. This guide takes you from that first request to a recipe you can keep.

**The short version:** describe your meal → select Generate → read the recipe → save it for later.

## 1. Start with a craving, ingredients, or a cooking project

Open the [recipe generator](/generate) and use the box marked “What are you craving?” Start with whichever of these fits your kitchen today.

| Your starting point             | Example request                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| You know what you want          | “A bacon double cheeseburger with crispy onion rings.”                                                 |
| You have ingredients to use     | “What can I make with chickpeas, spinach, and potatoes?”                                               |
| You want to learn a method      | “How do I make tofu from dried soybeans?”                                                              |
| You want a from-scratch project | “Homemade seitan sweet-and-sour pork, starting with whole-wheat flour and using a cheesecloth method.” |

These are starting ideas, not a special format to memorize. A simple dish name is fine; add more detail when the method or ingredients matter to you.

The app is designed to make vegan recipes. You can name a familiar dish without having to work out every plant-based ingredient yourself first.

## 2. Add the details that would change your dinner

Tell the app about the limits that matter: time, servings, equipment, or ingredients you want to use or avoid. You don’t need to fill in every detail for every meal.

For example:

> “Creamy mushroom pasta for two, without cashews. I have oat milk, garlic, and dried spaghetti.”

Or:

> “A spicy tofu dinner with broccoli and peanut butter. About 30 minutes, using a frying pan—no oven.”

You can describe the result you’re after, too: crispy edges, a thick sauce, a filling sandwich, or something mild enough for your tastes.

Vegan doesn’t mean gluten-free. Ask for gluten-free food when you need it; there’s no reason to add that restriction just because you’re cooking without animal products.

If a method matters, name it. “Make the seitan from whole-wheat flour” is a different request from “use ready-made seitan.”

## 3. Generate, then read before you cook

Select **Generate** when your request is ready. VeganGenius Chef produces a recipe with measured ingredients, a step-by-step method, servings, and prep and cooking times.

Before starting, read the ingredients and the full method. Check that the result fits your request and that you have the equipment it calls for. Treat the timing as a planning estimate, not a promise.

Recipes are AI-generated, not automatically kitchen-tested. Check packaged ingredients for animal-derived ingredients and allergens. A dietary request is not an allergy-safety guarantee.

The food image is AI-generated too. It illustrates the recipe; it isn’t proof that someone cooked or tested the dish. Use the written steps and your own observations rather than aiming to copy the image exactly.

## 4. Keep the recipes you want to make again

Save a recipe you like, then return to it through [My Kitchen](/kitchen). Use **New Cookbook** to organize your collection around the way you cook: “Weeknight dinners,” “Comfort food,” or “Try next.”

Choose how you want to keep your recipes:

- **Guest:** your cookbook stays in the browser you’re using.
- **Google sign-in:** your cookbook can sync across your devices.

An account isn’t required to get started. If you want to move between a laptop and a phone, use Google sign-in rather than expecting a guest collection to follow you.

## 5. Browse when you don’t know what to ask for

Open [Browse](/browse) for published recipes and ideas. Choose a category, such as dinner, tofu, or comfort food, or use **Sort** and **Apply** to change the order.

Public recipe pages include a **Save to your cookbook** link. You can also publish recipes from your own collection to the public catalog when you want to share them.

Browsing and generating are different starting points. Use a published recipe when something catches your eye, or return to the generator with an idea of your own.

## A couple of useful things to know

Generating, saving, and browsing are free. An hourly fair-use limit applies to generation; if you reach it, follow the message shown and try again later.

Nutrition figures aren’t calculated by the app. The [About page](/about) explains more about the recipes and the project.

Ready? Start with something you actually want to eat. You don’t need a perfect prompt to try VeganGenius Chef—just a craving, a few ingredients, or a question about cooking.
